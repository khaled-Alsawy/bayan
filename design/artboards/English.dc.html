YOUR QUESTION

Which verses speak about protecting a person from envy and the evil eye?

Written reading — composed from the sources shown below, not quoted from them

The Qur'an addresses this from one direct angle: seeking refuge with God from the harm of an envier. Sūrat al-Falaq closes by naming four harms, the last of them envyAl-Falaq 5which makes it the clearest Qur'anic locus on the subject.

The sūrah is an act of refuge-seeking throughout: it opens by turning to the Lord of the daybreakAl-Falaq 1then enumerates what is sought refuge from, item by item, so that envy arrives as the closing term rather than the opening one.

Partial coverage The Qur'an answers the part about seeking refuge from an envier. The practical means of protection come from the Sunnah, which is outside this tool's scope for now.

Evidence

Sūrat al-Falaq in full, and an excerpt from Ibn Kathīr

Sūrat al-Falaq

Verses 1–5 · Madīnah muṣḥaf, Ḥafṣ ʿan ʿĀṣim

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ١مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ٢وَمِن شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ٣وَمِن شَرِّ ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ فِى ٱلْعُقَدِ٤وَمِن شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ٥

TRANSLATION · ROWWAD TRANSLATION CENTER
  1. Say, “I seek refuge with the Lord of the daybreak
  2. from the evil of what He has created,
  3. and from the evil of the darkening night when it settles,
  4. and from the evil of those who blow on knots,
  5. and from the evil of an envier when he envies.”
